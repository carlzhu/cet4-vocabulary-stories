# Character Guide

Status: **curriculum baseline approved for planning**

## Main cast

### Lin Wei (林伟) — primary viewpoint

- Starts as an 18-year-old first-year environmental-engineering student at Harbor City University.
- Careful, observant, and persistent, but initially avoids disagreement and public speaking.
- Long arc: learns to study deliberately, lead a mixed team, accept responsibility, and choose socially useful work over a safer but empty career path.
- Language constraint: clear, thoughtful narration; growth is shown through decisions rather than repeated self-description.

### Maya Chen (陈曼雅) — friend and co-lead

- Journalism and digital-media student raised in another province.
- Curious, direct, socially confident, and quick to notice whose voice is missing.
- Long arc: turns a campus reporting assignment into a public-interest media project while learning to distinguish speed from accuracy.
- Relationship with Lin Wei: constructive disagreement, mutual trust, no forced romance.

### Aisha Rahman — exchange student and data specialist

- Computer-science student who joins during the culture-exchange arc and remains a remote collaborator afterward.
- Analytical, humorous, and sensitive to cultural assumptions.
- Long arc: helps the group build technology responsibly and challenges designs that exclude ordinary users.

### Daniel Brooks — athlete and community organizer

- Sports-science student and member of the university rowing team.
- Energetic and dependable, but hides stress until it affects his health.
- Long arc: connects physical health, mental health, teamwork, and accessible community programs.

### Professor Zhao (赵教授) — academic mentor

- Teaches environmental systems and project design.
- Demanding about evidence, source quality, and unintended consequences.
- Never solves the students' problems for them; asks questions that force revision.

### Grace Liu (刘佳) — workplace mentor

- Harbor City University alumna and product manager at a civic-technology company.
- Introduced during the internship arc.
- Long arc: demonstrates ethical professional judgment and later offers Lin Wei a difficult career choice.

## Relationships and continuity

- Lin Wei and Maya meet during freshman orientation and form the core partnership.
- Daniel joins through a campus volunteer event; his health arc later changes the team's priorities.
- Aisha first collaborates during an overseas exchange and continues online across later chapters.
- Professor Zhao supervises the academic project but does not control the students' media, startup, or community decisions.
- Grace bridges university work and employment; she is a mentor, not a substitute protagonist.
- Family members appear when decisions have real personal costs, not as disposable lesson topics.

## Continuity rules

1. Ages, academic years, locations, skills, and established relationships must remain consistent.
2. Each chapter begins from a consequence of the previous chapter; no repeated cast introductions.
3. Technical, health, economic, and environmental claims require plausible evidence inside the story.
4. Conflict must arise from goals, constraints, or values—not arbitrary misunderstandings.
5. Target vocabulary serves the scene; scenes must not become disguised word lists.
